# Disease Guide: Potato, Maize and Bell Pepper (Capsicum)

Compiled for AgriMind AI demonstration from standard extension recommendations. Confirm chemical choices and doses with the product label and local experts.

## Potato

### Early blight of potato (Alternaria solani)
Symptoms: dark brown circular spots with concentric rings on older leaves, usually after mid-season; leaves yellow and die early, reducing tuber size.
Management: balanced nutrition (stressed, nitrogen-deficient plants are more affected); rotation; remove volunteer plants; protectant fungicide sprays such as mancozeb or chlorothalonil at the label dose.

### Late blight of potato (Phytophthora infestans)
Symptoms: pale green, water-soaked spots at leaf tips and margins that rapidly turn brown-black; white mildew on the leaf underside in humid mornings; brown dry rot in tubers. It is the most destructive potato disease and can destroy a crop within 1–2 weeks in cool, wet weather.
Management: plant certified disease-free seed tubers; use resistant varieties released by ICAR-CPRI (for example Kufri Jyoti or Kufri Girdhari in hill areas); earth up the rows to protect tubers; follow disease forecasts; spray mancozeb as a protectant and switch to systemic combinations such as metalaxyl + mancozeb or dimethomorph-based products when blight appears; destroy haulms 10–15 days before harvest.

### Healthy potato foliage
Healthy leaves are uniformly green without spots, yellowing or wilting. Keep monitoring at least weekly, particularly after rain.

## Maize (corn)

### Common rust (Puccinia sorghi)
Symptoms: small, oval to elongated, cinnamon-brown powdery pustules scattered on both leaf surfaces; pustules turn darker as they mature. Favoured by moderate temperatures (16–23 °C) and high humidity.
Management: grow resistant hybrids; timely sowing; a foliar fungicide (e.g. mancozeb or a triazole/strobilurin product) is economical only on susceptible hybrids when infection starts before tasseling.

### Northern corn leaf blight / Turcicum leaf blight (Exserohilum turcicum)
Symptoms: long (2.5–15 cm), cigar-shaped, grey-green to tan lesions on leaves, starting on lower leaves. Severe infection before silking can cause large yield loss. Favoured by moderate temperatures and heavy dew.
Management: resistant hybrids; crop rotation and residue management; fungicide sprays at early disease appearance in susceptible hybrids.

### Gray leaf spot / Cercospora leaf spot (Cercospora zeae-maydis)
Symptoms: rectangular, grey to tan lesions bounded by leaf veins, giving a "matchstick" appearance; lesions merge and blight the leaf. Favoured by warm, humid weather and continuous maize with surface residue.
Management: rotation, tillage to bury residue, resistant hybrids, and fungicides when needed.

### Fall armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda)
A major insect pest of maize in Karnataka since 2018. Larvae feed in the whorl, leaving ragged holes and sawdust-like excreta; older larvae have an inverted "Y" mark on the head.
Management: early sowing in a locality at the same time; inspect whorls twice a week; install pheromone traps; apply sand mixed with lime into the whorl in early stages; spray neem oil (1500 ppm) or approved insecticides (e.g. emamectin benzoate, spinetoram, chlorantraniliprole) into the whorl at recommended doses when 10% of plants are damaged.

### Healthy maize leaves
Healthy leaves are dark green with no lesions or pustules. Pale yellow leaves starting from the tip of older leaves usually indicate nitrogen deficiency rather than disease.

## Bell pepper / Capsicum

### Bacterial spot of pepper (Xanthomonas euvesicatoria and related species)
Symptoms: small, water-soaked spots on leaves that turn brown with yellow halos; leaves turn yellow and drop, exposing fruit to sunscald; raised, scabby spots on fruit. Spread by infected seed, rain splash and handling wet plants.
Management: use certified, treated seed and healthy transplants; avoid overhead irrigation; 2–3 year rotation away from pepper and tomato; copper-based sprays reduce spread; remove crop debris after harvest.

### Healthy pepper plants
Healthy pepper leaves are glossy green. Curling and puckering of leaves can be caused by thrips, mites or viruses rather than bacterial spot and should be checked separately.

## General rule for leaf-image diagnosis
A photo of a single leaf can suggest a likely disease, but similar symptoms can come from nutrient deficiency, herbicide injury, water stress or other pathogens. Inspect the whole field, check several plants, and confirm with an expert before spending on chemicals.
