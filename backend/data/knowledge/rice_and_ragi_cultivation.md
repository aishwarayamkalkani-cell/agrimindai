# Rice (Paddy) and Finger Millet (Ragi) Cultivation in Karnataka

Compiled for AgriMind AI demonstration from standard package-of-practice recommendations. Varieties and doses differ by agro-climatic zone; consult the University of Agricultural Sciences (UAS) package of practices for your zone.

## Rice (paddy)

### Seasons
Main crop is Kharif, sown in June–July with the south-west monsoon. Summer (rabi/summer) rice is grown under canal and tank irrigation from December–January in command areas such as Tungabhadra, Bhadra and Cauvery.

### Nursery and transplanting
Use 50–60 kg seed per hectare for transplanting (lower for SRI). Treat seed with a fungicide such as carbendazim or with Pseudomonas fluorescens. Transplant 25–30 day old seedlings of medium-duration varieties at 2–3 seedlings per hill with 20 × 15 cm spacing (about 33 hills per square metre).

### System of Rice Intensification (SRI)
SRI uses young seedlings (8–12 days), one seedling per hill at a wide 25 × 25 cm square spacing, intermittent irrigation instead of continuous flooding, mechanical weeding with a cono-weeder and more organic manure. It needs only 5–7.5 kg seed per hectare and saves water.

### Nutrients
Apply 10 t/ha farmyard manure or green manure. A typical recommendation for irrigated paddy is about 100 kg N, 50 kg P2O5 and 50 kg K2O per hectare: all P and K plus half the N at transplanting, and the remaining N in two splits at active tillering and panicle initiation. Apply zinc sulphate (about 20–25 kg/ha) on zinc-deficient soils. Leaf colour charts help decide nitrogen top-dressing.

### Water
Keep 2–5 cm of standing water during most of the crop; maintain water during panicle initiation and flowering; drain the field 10–15 days before harvest.

### Major problems
- **Blast** (Magnaporthe oryzae): spindle-shaped spots with grey centres and brown margins on leaves; neck blast causes the panicle to break and grains to remain empty. Favoured by high nitrogen, cloudy humid weather and cool nights. Manage with resistant varieties, balanced nitrogen and recommended fungicides such as tricyclazole.
- **Bacterial leaf blight** (Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae): yellow to white wavy lesions from the leaf tip downwards. Avoid excess nitrogen, drain flooded fields, use resistant varieties.
- **Brown planthopper**: insects at the base of plants cause circular patches of dried plants ("hopperburn"). Avoid excess nitrogen, leave alleyways of 30 cm every 2–3 m, and use selective insecticides only when needed.
- **Stem borer**: "dead heart" at the vegetative stage and "white ear" (empty white panicle) at heading. Use pheromone traps and egg parasitoid Trichogramma japonicum releases.

### Harvest
Harvest when 80–85% of grains in the panicle turn straw-coloured. Dry grain to about 12–14% moisture before storage.

## Finger millet (ragi)
Ragi is Karnataka's most important millet and the state is the largest producer in India. It is hardy, drought-tolerant and rich in calcium, iron and dietary fibre.

### Season and sowing
Mainly a rain-fed Kharif crop sown from July to August in southern Karnataka (earlier in some areas). It is also grown under irrigation in summer. Sow 10 kg seed/ha in lines 30 cm apart (or 22.5 cm in late sowing), with plants 7.5–10 cm apart; transplanting of 21–25 day old seedlings is also practised. Varieties released by UAS Bengaluru (for example the GPU series and MR series) are widely grown.

### Nutrients
Apply 7.5–10 t/ha farmyard manure. A typical rain-fed recommendation is about 50 kg N, 40 kg P2O5 and 25 kg K2O per hectare; irrigated crops need more. Seed treatment with Azospirillum and phosphate-solubilising bacteria improves nutrient uptake.

### Main disease: blast
Finger millet blast (Magnaporthe grisea) causes leaf, neck and finger blast and is the most damaging disease. Use resistant varieties, treat seed with fungicides, and spray recommended fungicides at flowering if neck blast appears.

### Advantages
Ragi grows on poor, shallow red soils with 500–900 mm of rainfall where paddy is not possible, stores for many years without pest damage, and its straw is good fodder.
