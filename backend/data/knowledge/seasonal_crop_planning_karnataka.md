# Seasonal Crop Planning for Karnataka

Compiled for AgriMind AI demonstration. Use the AgriMind crop-planning page for district-level historical production statistics.

## Cropping seasons in India
- **Kharif (monsoon)**: sown with the onset of the south-west monsoon (June–July) and harvested September–November. Typical crops: rice, maize, ragi, jowar, bajra, tur (pigeon pea), green gram, black gram, groundnut, soybean, cotton, sunflower.
- **Rabi (post-monsoon/winter)**: sown October–November on residual moisture or with irrigation, harvested February–April. Typical crops: wheat, chickpea (Bengal gram), rabi jowar, safflower, linseed, mustard, onion and other vegetables.
- **Zaid / summer**: sown February–March under irrigation and harvested before the monsoon. Typical crops: watermelon, muskmelon, cucumber, vegetables, green gram, summer groundnut and fodder crops.

## Karnataka agro-climatic regions (simplified)
- **Coastal Karnataka** (Dakshina Kannada, Udupi, Uttara Kannada): very high rainfall (over 3000 mm), laterite soils; rice, arecanut, coconut, cashew, black pepper.
- **Malnad / hill zone** (Shivamogga, Chikkamagaluru, Kodagu, Hassan hills): high rainfall, acidic red-laterite soils; coffee, black pepper, cardamom, arecanut, rice.
- **Southern dry and transition zones** (Bengaluru Rural, Kolar, Tumakuru, Mandya, Mysuru, Chamarajanagar): 600–900 mm rain, red sandy loam soils; ragi, field bean, groundnut, pulses, vegetables, mulberry (sericulture), sugarcane and paddy under canal irrigation.
- **Northern dry zone** (Vijayapura, Bagalkot, Kalaburagi, Raichur, Ballari, Koppal): low rainfall (500–700 mm), black cotton (vertisol) soils; jowar, tur, Bengal gram, sunflower, cotton, bajra; paddy in Tungabhadra and Upper Krishna command areas.
- **North-eastern zone** (Bidar, Kalaburagi): tur, soybean, green gram, black gram.

## Principles for choosing a crop
1. **Water**: match crop water need to rainfall and irrigation. Rice and sugarcane need assured water; ragi, jowar, bajra, tur and horse gram suit rain-fed fields.
2. **Soil**: deep black soils hold moisture for rabi crops such as chickpea and rabi jowar; light red soils suit ragi, groundnut and field bean; acidic soils suit coffee, tea and pepper; saline or sodic soils need tolerant crops (e.g. barley, cotton) and reclamation.
3. **Market and price**: check mandi prices and storage facilities before sowing perishable crops such as tomato and onion; staggered sowing avoids price crashes at harvest.
4. **Rotation**: alternate cereals with pulses (which fix nitrogen) to improve soil fertility and break pest cycles; avoid growing tomato, potato, brinjal and chilli (all Solanaceae) back to back.
5. **Risk**: intercropping (e.g. ragi + tur at 8:2 or 10:2 rows, groundnut + tur) and crop insurance under PMFBY reduce risk from failed monsoon.

## Contingency planning for delayed monsoon
If the monsoon is delayed by 2–4 weeks, choose shorter-duration varieties, crops such as green gram, horse gram, cowpea and fodder crops, or transplant ragi from a nursery raised with protective irrigation. Follow the district contingency plan published by ICAR-CRIDA and the state agricultural universities.
