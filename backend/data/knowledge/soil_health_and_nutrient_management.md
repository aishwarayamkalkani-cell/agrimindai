# Soil Health and Nutrient Management

Compiled for AgriMind AI demonstration from standard soil-science references used in Indian soil testing laboratories.

## Why test soil
A soil test tells how much of each nutrient is available, the soil reaction (pH) and salt level (EC). Applying fertiliser according to a soil test avoids both deficiency and waste. Under the Soil Health Card scheme, farmers get free tests covering 12 parameters: nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium (K), sulphur (S), zinc (Zn), iron (Fe), copper (Cu), manganese (Mn), boron (B), pH, electrical conductivity (EC) and organic carbon (OC).

## How to take a soil sample
Collect samples after harvest and before applying fertiliser. Take 10–15 sub-samples from a zig-zag path across the field at 0–15 cm depth (deeper, 0–30 cm, for orchards), avoiding bunds, compost pits and tree shade. Mix them, reduce to about 500 g by quartering, dry in shade and label with the farmer's name and survey number.

## Commonly used fertility ratings (available nutrients)
| Parameter | Low | Medium | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Available nitrogen (kg/ha) | below 280 | 280–560 | above 560 |
| Available phosphorus (kg/ha, as P) | below 10 | 10–25 | above 25 |
| Available potassium (kg/ha, as K) | below 110 | 110–280 | above 280 |
| Organic carbon (%) | below 0.5 | 0.5–0.75 | above 0.75 |

For a "low" rating, fertiliser doses are usually increased by about 25% over the general recommendation; for "high", they are reduced by about 25%. Follow the dose on your Soil Health Card where available.

## Soil pH (soil reaction)
| pH | Class | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| below 5.5 | Strongly acidic | Phosphorus fixation, aluminium/iron toxicity possible; apply agricultural lime as per lime requirement test |
| 5.5–6.5 | Moderately to slightly acidic | Suitable for most crops; tea, coffee, potato and pineapple tolerate acidity |
| 6.5–7.5 | Neutral | Ideal for most crops; nutrients most available |
| 7.5–8.5 | Alkaline | Zinc, iron and phosphorus availability falls; use organic manures and acid-forming fertilisers |
| above 8.5 | Strongly alkaline / sodic | Needs reclamation with gypsum according to gypsum requirement, plus drainage |

## Electrical conductivity (EC, dS/m, 1:2 soil:water)
Below 1.0 is normal; 1.0–2.0 is critical for salt-sensitive crops (beans, many vegetables); 2.0–3.0 is injurious to sensitive crops; above 3.0 is injurious to most crops. Saline soils need leaching with good-quality water and improved drainage.

## Role of major nutrients and deficiency symptoms
- **Nitrogen (N)**: leaf and stem growth, green colour. Deficiency: uniform pale yellowing that starts on older (lower) leaves, stunted growth. Excess: lush growth, lodging and more pest attack.
- **Phosphorus (P)**: root development, flowering and seed formation. Deficiency: purplish or dark green older leaves, poor rooting, delayed maturity. Apply P as a basal dose because it moves little in soil.
- **Potassium (K)**: water regulation, disease resistance, grain and fruit quality. Deficiency: scorching and browning along the margins of older leaves, weak stems.
- **Sulphur (S)**: yellowing of younger leaves (unlike N, which shows on older leaves); important for oilseeds and pulses.
- **Zinc (Zn)**: common deficiency in Indian soils; "khaira" disease in rice with brown spots and stunting; white bud in maize. Corrected with zinc sulphate applied to soil (often 25 kg/ha) or as a foliar spray.
- **Boron (B)**: hollow stems and cracking in vegetables, poor fruit set; needed in very small amounts.

## Common fertilisers and nutrient content
- Urea: 46% N
- Di-ammonium phosphate (DAP): 18% N, 46% P2O5
- Single super phosphate (SSP): 16% P2O5, about 11% S
- Muriate of potash (MOP): 60% K2O
- Ammonium sulphate: 20.6% N, 24% S
To convert P to P2O5 multiply by 2.29; to convert K to K2O multiply by 1.2.

## Organic matter and soil biology
Organic carbon below 0.5% is common in Indian soils. Raise it with farmyard manure, compost or vermicompost (5–10 t/ha), green manuring with sunhemp or dhaincha incorporated at flowering, crop residue retention, and legumes in rotation. Biofertilisers — Rhizobium for pulses, Azospirillum or Azotobacter for cereals, and phosphate-solubilising bacteria — reduce chemical fertiliser need and are cheap.

## Integrated nutrient management (INM)
Combine organic manures, biofertilisers and chemical fertilisers based on soil test values. Apply nitrogen in splits matched to crop demand, place fertiliser near the root zone rather than broadcasting on the surface, and avoid applying fertiliser just before heavy rain.
