# Integrated Pest Management (IPM), Safe Pesticide Use and Organic Practices

Compiled for AgriMind AI demonstration from standard IPM guidance (Directorate of Plant Protection, Quarantine & Storage and state agricultural universities).

## IPM principles
IPM combines several methods to keep pests below the level that causes economic loss, using chemicals only as a last resort:
1. **Prevention (cultural methods)**: healthy seed, resistant varieties, summer ploughing to expose pest stages, crop rotation, timely sowing, optimum spacing, balanced fertilisation (excess nitrogen increases sucking pests and many diseases), field sanitation and removal of crop residues.
2. **Monitoring**: walk the field weekly in a zig-zag pattern, use pheromone traps, yellow and blue sticky traps and light traps, and record pest counts.
3. **Economic threshold level (ETL)**: spray only when pest numbers cross the threshold at which control costs less than the expected loss.
4. **Mechanical and physical control**: hand-picking egg masses and larvae, insect-proof nets in nurseries, bird perches (about 10 per acre) for caterpillar control.
5. **Biological control**: conserve natural enemies (ladybird beetles, spiders, lacewings), release Trichogramma egg parasitoids, use biopesticides such as Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt), NPV (nuclear polyhedrosis virus), Beauveria bassiana, Metarhizium, Trichoderma and Pseudomonas fluorescens.
6. **Chemical control**: choose the recommended, least toxic product; rotate chemicals with different modes of action to prevent resistance.

## Botanical pesticides
- Neem seed kernel extract (NSKE) 5%: soak 5 kg of powdered neem seed kernel overnight in 10 litres of water, filter, dilute to 100 litres and add a little soap as sticker. Effective against many caterpillars and sucking pests as an antifeedant and repellent.
- Commercial neem oil formulations (1500–10000 ppm azadirachtin) are used at the label dose.

## Safe use of pesticides
- Read the label: dose, target pest, waiting period (pre-harvest interval) and colour-coded toxicity triangle (red = extremely toxic, yellow = highly toxic, blue = moderately toxic, green = slightly toxic).
- Wear gloves, mask, full sleeves and boots; never eat, drink or smoke while spraying.
- Spray in the calm early morning or evening, never against the wind.
- Do not spray during flowering when bees are active, or close to water bodies.
- Do not mix chemicals unless the label says they are compatible.
- Store pesticides locked away from food and children; triple-rinse, puncture and safely dispose of empty containers.
- Respect the waiting period between the last spray and harvest, especially for vegetables.
- In case of poisoning, move the person to fresh air, remove contaminated clothes, and take them with the pesticide label to the nearest hospital.

## Organic farming practices
- **Composting**: layer crop residues, green material and cow dung in a pit or heap, keep moist and turn every 2–3 weeks; compost is ready in 2–4 months.
- **Vermicompost**: earthworms (Eisenia fetida) convert dung and residues into rich manure in 45–60 days under shade with 40–50% moisture.
- **Jeevamrutha**: a fermented microbial culture prepared from cow dung, cow urine, jaggery, pulse flour and a handful of soil in water, used to stimulate soil microbial activity.
- **Green manuring**: grow sunhemp or dhaincha and incorporate at flowering (about 45 days) to add organic matter and nitrogen.
- **Crop diversity**: intercropping (e.g. ragi with field bean or pigeon pea in Karnataka), border crops and trap crops reduce pest outbreaks.
- Certification: Participatory Guarantee System (PGS-India) for groups of farmers, or third-party certification under NPOP for export markets. Conversion takes at least 2–3 years.
