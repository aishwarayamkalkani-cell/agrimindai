# Irrigation and Water Management

Compiled for AgriMind AI demonstration. Crop coefficients follow FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper No. 56 (Allen et al., 1998).

## Estimating crop water need
Crop water requirement (ETc) is estimated as ETc = Kc × ET0, where ET0 is reference evapotranspiration (mm/day) from weather data and Kc is the crop coefficient for the growth stage. Net irrigation need is ETc minus effective rainfall. Gross irrigation = net irrigation ÷ application efficiency.

Typical application efficiency: surface/flood irrigation 50–60%, sprinkler 70–80%, drip 85–95%.

The initial stage lasts from sowing until about 10% ground cover; mid-season runs from full cover through flowering and grain or fruit filling, and is when the crop uses the most water.

## Critical stages for irrigation
If water is limited, irrigate at the stages where stress reduces yield the most:
- Rice: tillering, panicle initiation, flowering.
- Maize: tasseling and silking (most critical), then grain filling.
- Wheat: crown root initiation (about 21 days after sowing), flowering.
- Groundnut: pegging and pod development.
- Tomato and capsicum: flowering and fruit enlargement; irregular water causes fruit cracking and blossom-end rot.
- Pulses: flowering and pod filling.

## Drip irrigation
Drip delivers water at the root zone, saving 30–60% water compared with flood irrigation, reducing weeds and foliar disease, and allowing fertigation (feeding water-soluble fertilisers with irrigation). It suits vegetables, sugarcane, banana, orchards and plantation crops. Filters must be cleaned regularly and laterals flushed; acid treatment removes salt deposits from emitters.

## Sprinkler irrigation
Suitable for close-spaced crops such as groundnut, pulses, wheat and leafy vegetables on undulating land. Avoid sprinklers during strong wind and in the hottest part of the day.

## Practical scheduling tips
- Check soil moisture by hand: if a ball of soil from the root zone crumbles, irrigate; if it sticks together and leaves the palm wet, wait.
- Irrigate in the early morning or evening to reduce evaporation.
- Mulching with crop residue or plastic film reduces evaporation and weeds.
- In paddy, alternate wetting and drying (AWD) saves 15–30% water: let the water level fall to about 15 cm below the soil surface (checked with a perforated field tube) before re-flooding, except during flowering.
- Farm ponds collect runoff for protective irrigation during dry spells in rain-fed areas.
